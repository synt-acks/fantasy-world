# 04 — Chapter Blueprint: *The Road Beyond Hazelwood*

## Status and use

**NEW CANON:** Title and approved A+B direction. **PLANNED:** All chapter events below. Each chapter uses third-person limited viewpoint through Philip. This is a scene roadmap, not prose or a requirement to preserve every line during drafting. See `03_STORY_BIBLE.md` for full spoilers, rules, and stable clue IDs.

## Act I — Leaving Hazelwood

### 1. River Rope

- **Setting / timeline:** Hazelwood dock and the family shop, Day 1 morning and evening.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants to get home from school without another beating. Seven classmates trap and throw him into the Big River. He spots a bank rope and uses it to climb out.
- **Character decision:** He tells Father the truth rather than pretending he fell. Father responds with an order to fight back, not a plan for how to do so.
- **World / magic:** Establish river orientation, dock rope, local shop, and the giant rumor. No active magic yet.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-01 and F-02. The rope is useful because Philip tests its route; Father’s definition of strength begins their conflict.
- **Tone:** Immediate danger with a brief practical observation after the rescue; no jokes about the bullying.
- **Ending / link:** Father says a giant has left the mountain. Philip assumes the forest is somewhere he can avoid.

### 2. The Arrow Seller

- **Setting / timeline:** Hazelwood shop, Day 1 afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants to serve a remarkable customer without appearing nervous. Aro Silvet arrives in his battered Alcrog hide and buys the elven arrows.
- **Important events:** Aro tells the story of his dead friend Philip and the Alcrog. He asks about the boy’s name and says the young Philip looks like a warrior. Father and Aro speak privately in Elvish about a damaged north-road marker and the giant’s passage.
- **Character decision:** Father decides to advance the family trip to Malcore. Aro will travel separately and report the warning there.
- **World / magic:** Introduce hunters, elves’ arrows, Alcrogs, and the once-active Slayers through customer conversation, not a lore lecture.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-04 and F-05; M-01 begins. The story is not prophecy. The iron fittings are present but Philip does not know their use.
- **Ending / link:** Aro leaves before Philip can learn what the private conversation meant.

### 3. The Early Trip

- **Setting / timeline:** Shop and home, evening; departure before dawn on Day 2.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants an explanation for the sudden trip and to be treated as capable of understanding it. Father calls the journey a needed break and shows him the locked dwarven box without opening it.
- **Important events:** Philip says he prefers the shop and does not want to be made into a fighter. Father says he must be strong. Philip notices that Father is worried, not merely impatient.
- **Decision:** Philip packs and goes, but does not accept the implied future Father has in mind.
- **World / magic:** Establish their past Malcore supply trips, Father’s old Slayer connection, and the box/key.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-02, F-03, M-01. The knife is a gift tied to Father’s departure, not a summons.
- **Ending / link:** Father takes the box himself. At dawn they begin the southbound journey.

### 4. Shelf Road

- **Setting / timeline:** Old South Road above the crash ravine, Day 2 before midday.
- **Objective and conflict:** Father wants to reach Malcore before worsening weather; Philip wants the trip to be more than a long ride and asks about school and shop plans.
- **Important events:** Philip notices cracked drainage and an old iron road fitting. Father sees the warning but continues because the modern route is longer and the giant warning feels urgent. A weakened road shoulder collapses under the wagon. Father pushes Philip onto a safer shelf; the wagon falls and burns below.
- **Decision:** Father saves Philip at the cost of a serious injury to himself.
- **World / magic:** Show old road construction, a waystone’s ordinary appearance, and the practical effects of neglected maintenance. The collapse is caused by erosion and earlier heavy ground pressure, not sabotage or magic.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-05; the wreck gives Philip the tool and route problem he must later understand.
- **Ending / link:** Philip blacks out, then wakes alone beside the wreckage.

### 5. Wreckage

- **Setting / timeline:** Crash clearing, Day 2 afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip must determine whether Father is alive and move him away from fire and unstable debris while Philip is injured himself.
- **Important events:** He finds Father breathing, checks for obvious danger, and improvises a basic wrap for his own head wound. He cannot move Father far and realizes the wagon is no longer a source of supplies.
- **Decision:** He stays with Father instead of running toward the road alone.
- **World / magic:** Physical survival details and wreckage; no convenient supplies appear.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-03; the box survives because it is well made, not invulnerable magic.
- **Tone:** Fear and quiet practical focus.
- **Ending / link:** In the dying firelight Philip finds the box’s handle under a broken plank.

### 6. The Knife in the Box

- **Setting / timeline:** Crash clearing and nearby old marker, Day 2 dusk.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants to understand the gift and find a way out before dark.
- **Important events:** He uses Father’s key to open the dwarven box. Inside is the finely made dagger. While searching for a path, he brings the dagger near a cracked marker; it vibrates and pulls faintly toward the trees. Philip assumes it points to the road and cannot tell whether the reading is real.
- **Decision:** He puts the knife away and rests instead of following an unexplained sensation while injured.
- **World / magic:** First sensory demonstration of Waykeeping, but no explanation. Keep the vibration small and ambiguous.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-03, F-05; M-02 begins.
- **Ending / link:** Philip sleeps beside Father and the unopened question of the knife.

### 7. The Berry Tree

- **Setting / timeline:** Crash clearing and forest edge, Day 2 night into Day 3 dawn.
- **Objective and conflict:** Hungry, Philip needs food and must keep his strength for Father.
- **Important events:** He chases a squirrel, fails, then climbs for berries through trial and error. He hurts his hand and falls once. He returns with enough food to share and chooses to stop before exhaustion makes another fall likely.
- **Character development:** He proves to himself that he can learn a physical task, but does not conclude he can do anything without help.
- **World / magic:** Forest berries, local animals, and the first distant tremor from the giant.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-08 begins as a tremor disturbs animal behavior.
- **Tone:** A wry note about heroic plans meeting a branch that will not cooperate.
- **Ending / link:** Dawn reveals that the road back to the north is damaged.

## Act II — Reading the Forest

### 8. A Stretcher and a Choice

- **Setting / timeline:** Crash clearing and north road, Day 3 morning.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants to move Father without worsening his injury. Dragging him fails; Philip builds a branch-and-cloth stretcher.
- **Important events:** He inspects the northward road and finds a landslide over the narrow shelf. The river has risen and is unsafe to cross. Father briefly wakes, gives a name and a direction—Malcore, downstream, where there is a healer—then fades.
- **Decision:** Philip chooses the less familiar southbound road instead of waiting for a rescue or attempting the blocked way home.
- **Character development:** He accepts that a good decision can be frightening and incomplete.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-01; his route choice is his own, even though Malcore was Father’s original destination.
- **Ending / link:** Philip takes the stretcher toward the forest road; a fox watches from the trees.

### 9. The Fox at the Treeline

- **Setting / timeline:** Forest approach, Day 3 midday.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants to learn whether the fox can help with food or the route; the fox refuses to be commanded.
- **Important events:** The fox pushes the stretcher when Philip stumbles, then speaks and names himself Filroy Foxman. Philip calls him Mr. Fox. Filroy says animals are avoiding the giant’s path and that his own territory has been disrupted.
- **Decision:** Philip accepts help without requiring Filroy to promise more than he can do.
- **World / magic:** Speaking animal is an existing wonder; do not connect it to Waykeeping or explain it yet.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-07 begins; Filroy knows local dry ground and animal movement.
- **Tone:** Banter from their different priorities, with hunger and Father’s condition still present.
- **Ending / link:** Filroy points out a marked trail; Philip chooses to test it.

### 10. A Road with No Road

- **Setting / timeline:** Forest marker line and flooded crossing, Day 3 afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants a reliable route through a path obscured by brush and broken stones.
- **Important events:** He places the wayknife at an intact marker and experiences a strong pulse, a direction, and a brief overlay of heavy steps. He mistakes the strongest trace for the safest route and follows it to a washed crossing. Father’s stretcher catches; Philip must backtrack.
- **Decision:** He stops trusting the knife as an answer and asks Filroy what he sees on the ground.
- **Magic:** Demonstrate range, directional quality, sensory cost, and bias toward heavy repeated traffic.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-06, F-10; M-02’s central limitation is established before any payoff.
- **Ending / link:** Father groans and begins to wake; Philip has to explain his mistake.

### 11. Half Awake

- **Setting / timeline:** Dry rise above the swamp, Day 3 evening.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants Father’s help and an honest explanation. Father wants Philip to stay still and conserve strength.
- **Important events:** Father says the blade is a wayknife: it reads old traffic traces in road markers, and the strongest line is not necessarily safe. He gives the short version, not the Slayers’ history. Philip asks why Father gave him a tool he does not understand. Father says he planned to explain in Malcore.
- **Confrontation:** Philip tells him that being kept uninformed is not the same as being kept safe. Father replies that Philip is fifteen and hurt. Neither wins the argument.
- **Character movement:** Father has to accept Philip’s route observations; Philip admits he needs Father’s knowledge.
- **Clue / payoff:** M-02 advances; F-02 and F-03 reinforce the family conflict.
- **Ending / link:** Father sleeps again; Philip and Filroy choose a physical crossing rather than another marker reading.

### 12. The Swamp’s False Road

- **Setting / timeline:** Swamp edge and raised ground, Day 4 morning.
- **Objective and conflict:** Get Father across the swamp without losing the stretcher or exhausting Philip.
- **Important events:** A low path shown by the marker disappears under mud. Filroy finds a higher animal track. Philip tests each section with a branch before moving Father. They reach firm ground, but Father’s pain and thirst worsen.
- **Decision:** Philip uses observation and help instead of attempting to prove he can do everything alone.
- **World / magic:** Show that an animal trail and a marked road answer different needs; neither guarantees safe passage.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-01 and F-07; Philip begins to understand that route information is only one kind of knowledge.
- **Ending / link:** From a rise, Philip sees a broad damaged corridor through the forest.

### 13. The Giant’s Wake

- **Setting / timeline:** Hill above the damaged corridor, Day 4 midday.
- **Objective and conflict:** Determine whether the damaged forest means the giant is nearby and how to keep Father hidden.
- **Important events:** They see uprooted trees, crushed stone, and animal tracks leaving the area. They glimpse the giant at a distance, moving slowly along the corridor. It does not turn toward the stretcher.
- **Decision:** Philip chooses concealment and distance over trying to reach or attack it.
- **Character development:** He recognizes that fear can warn him without proving what the giant intends.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-08; the giant’s path looks repeatable, and its attention is on dry ground.
- **Tone:** Awe and fear; no comic aside during the first sighting.
- **Ending / link:** A new storm line rises behind them, making their current shelter temporary.

### 14. The Route Past Home

- **Setting / timeline:** Fork between the north track and the south road, Day 4 afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Decide whether to attempt the upstream route home or continue downstream to Malcore.
- **Important events:** Philip compares the blocked north track, swollen river, Father’s need for medical care, and the south road’s markers. Father wakes long enough to confirm Malcore is the better choice but still avoids saying why they were headed there.
- **Decision:** Philip commits to Malcore on his own assessment. He tells Father the decision is his, not an act of obedience.
- **Character development:** First clear expression of Philip’s agency.
- **Clue / payoff:** M-01 remains unresolved; F-02 changes from command to disagreement.
- **Ending / link:** The storm reaches the forest before they can reach the old bridge.

## Act III — The Old Road

### 15. Stormline

- **Setting / timeline:** Old bridge and forest slope, Day 4 evening.
- **Objective and conflict:** Keep the stretcher stable and shelter Father as severe rain and wind hit.
- **Important events:** Lightning strikes a tree; the storm is natural weather, not a magical attack. The bank gives way. Philip is thrown downhill while the stretcher is caught above the bridge.
- **Decision:** Philip returns uphill for Father instead of following the easier path to the road.
- **Magic / cost:** In panic he tries to read the wayknife and the movement echo worsens his disorientation. He puts it away.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-10; F-01 returns as a line is useful only if checked against the physical riverbank.
- **Ending / link:** Philip loses sight of Father in the rain.

### 16. Under the Rooted Bridge

- **Setting / timeline:** Shelter below the bridge, night into Day 5.
- **Objective and conflict:** Find Father before exposure and injury become fatal.
- **Important events:** Filroy locates the stretcher under a tree-rooted stone abutment. Philip treats Father’s closed leg fracture, checks his breathing, and keeps him dry. Father wakes confused; Philip must correct him without turning the moment into a lecture.
- **Character development:** Philip accepts Filroy’s leadership for the search. Father depends on Philip and recognizes it.
- **World:** The old bridge and road infrastructure show how routes survive after formal maintenance stops.
- **Ending / link:** Father can speak clearly enough to tell Philip why they were going to Malcore.

### 17. Father’s Road

- **Setting / timeline:** Bridge shelter, Day 5 dawn.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants the full reason for the trip before he risks another day on the road. Father wants to explain without frightening or burdening him.
- **Important events:** Father says Aro detected giant traffic at an old marker. The two intended to warn Malcore’s Road Office and review the route to Stillstone Hollow. Father also planned to give Philip the wayknife and discuss the Slayers after the danger was reported.
- **Confrontation:** Philip tells Father that he cannot choose whether to help if Father hides the situation. Father explains his fear that the old work will claim Philip as it claimed his own life and time with Philip’s mother. He does not say the raid was his fault.
- **Character movement:** They agree to share information and reassess decisions together, though trust is not yet repaired.
- **Clue / payoff:** M-01 resolves; M-04 becomes explicit.
- **Ending / link:** Aro’s tracks and a fresh marker reading show that the hunter is catching up.

### 18. The False Signal

- **Setting / timeline:** Raised road through the forest, Day 5 afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Aro catches up and wants to move ahead to prepare a kill site; Philip wants evidence before choosing a plan.
- **Important events:** Aro confirms he and Father agreed to meet at Malcore. He reads the strongest marker pulse as a direct line to the city. Philip notices the vibration is heavy and irregular, unlike cart traffic.
- **Decision:** Philip will not follow Aro’s order blindly, but agrees to share observations at Malcore.
- **Character conflict:** Father sides with Aro at first; Philip asks Father to hear his evidence.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-06 reinforces; M-03 is now an active question.
- **Ending / link:** They hear the giant moving in the direction of the river road.

### 19. The North Giant

- **Setting / timeline:** Dry ridge overlooking the giant’s route, Day 5 late afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Watch the giant without drawing it toward Father or the fox.
- **Important events:** The giant passes below. It turns away from a fox den and avoids a low flooded patch. When Aro’s gear clatters, it swings a fallen branch toward the noise, causing real danger without pursuing them.
- **Decision:** Philip chooses to observe from cover rather than attack or call out.
- **Character development:** He sees the difference between destructive force and deliberate pursuit.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-07 and F-08; the eastward dry rise is more important to the giant than people are.
- **Ending / link:** Aro still believes the city cannot wait for certainty; Father recognizes a faint old marker.

### 20. The Old Causeway

- **Setting / timeline:** Old causeway and a pair of route markers, Day 5 dusk.
- **Objective and conflict:** Reconstruct the route to Malcore without trusting one misleading marker.
- **Important events:** Father identifies the old causeway from memory. Philip compares three marker readings, notes that the giant’s recent heavy traffic masks an older eastward trace, and matches it to the land’s slope and dry ground.
- **Decision:** Philip proposes the eastward route as a possibility to test, not as a certainty.
- **Magic:** Demonstrate a logical application; the knife provides evidence, while physical checks establish direction.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-05, F-06, F-10; M-02 is substantially answered.
- **Ending / link:** The old route turns toward an unmarked area behind the city’s outer farms.

### 21. Aro’s Arrows

- **Setting / timeline:** Camp near the old causeway, Day 5 night.
- **Objective and conflict:** Agree whether Aro should prepare to shoot the giant if it reaches the city.
- **Important events:** Aro recounts more of his friend Philip’s Alcrog encounter and acknowledges that his friend died after the victory. Philip says he does not want to be measured against a dead namesake. Aro admits the story is a warning as much as a boast, though he still favors a lethal fallback.
- **Decision:** Aro agrees to wait for the route evidence before firing.
- **Relationship movement:** Philip and Aro shift from admiration and assumption to cautious respect.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-04; same name is a human coincidence, not fate.
- **Ending / link:** Malcore’s wall appears at first light; a warning bell announces the giant’s approach.

## Act IV — Malcore and the Choice

### 22. Malcore’s Gate

- **Setting / timeline:** Malcore’s outer gate, Day 6 morning.
- **Objective and conflict:** Get Father to medical help while the gate staff prepare for an approaching emergency.
- **Important events:** The main gate is closed and crowded. Philip notices a smaller wicket and a familiar mechanical groove, tests it, and releases its latch. The guards help carry Father to treatment. Filroy stays outside the city boundary.
- **Decision:** Philip enters even though the old fox disappears from sight; he prioritizes Father’s care.
- **World:** Gate design and old road connections; no magic opens the latch.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-09; Philip’s shopkeeper attention solves a small practical obstacle.
- **Ending / link:** The road office refuses to open the east service road until the route is verified.

### 23. The Road Office

- **Setting / timeline:** Malcore infirmary and road office, Day 6 midday.
- **Objective and conflict:** Keep Father under care while persuading officials to consider a nonlethal route plan.
- **Important events:** A healer stabilizes Father’s leg and concussion; recovery will take weeks. Aro argues that the giant must be stopped before it reaches the wall. The roadwarden explains that current records show the old east route blocked by farms and a drainage cut.
- **Decision:** Philip asks to examine the outer markers with a roadworker, rather than claiming the knife proves everything.
- **Character conflict:** Father tries to speak for Philip and is told he needs rest; this repeats their pattern in a smaller way.
- **Clue / payoff:** M-03 and M-04 bring the local historical dispute into the city.
- **Ending / link:** The roadwarden grants one short inspection before the city commits to an attack.

### 24. The Buried Turn

- **Setting / timeline:** East edge of Malcore, drainage cut and overgrown farm lane, Day 6 afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Find physical evidence that a route around Malcore is passable for the giant.
- **Important events:** Philip, Filroy, and a roadworker follow old stones. Filroy recognizes a familiar dry rise; Philip matches it to the fading route trace. They uncover an old turn behind a farm barrier and a drainage cut that can be cleared, but they cannot guarantee the giant will use it.
- **Decision:** Philip reports uncertainty honestly and asks for workers and evacuation, not blind faith in the magic.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-05 and F-07; Stillstone Hollow’s route becomes credible.
- **Ending / link:** The roadwarden authorizes a limited clearing crew; Father wants Philip sent away.

### 25. What Strength Costs

- **Setting / timeline:** Infirmary, Day 6 late afternoon.
- **Objective and conflict:** Philip wants to lead the route effort; Father wants him kept out of danger.
- **Important events:** Father tries to take the knife and send Philip to the inner city. Philip confronts him: Father told him to fight bullies, withheld the old road, and now wants him silent when he has useful evidence. Father admits he is afraid of another loss and that he mistook control for protection.
- **Decision:** Father gives Philip the knife back and agrees to support the route plan. Philip accepts Father’s fear without agreeing to be managed by it.
- **Emotional development:** This is the relationship’s peak confrontation, not a complete reconciliation.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-02 and F-03; the gift becomes a sign of trust rather than expectation.
- **Ending / link:** A bell sounds: the giant has reached the outer farms.

### 26. Broken Stones

- **Setting / timeline:** East service road, evening, Day 6.
- **Objective and conflict:** Clear the old route and verify enough of it for an evacuation.
- **Important events:** Workers move the farm barrier and clear a drainage obstruction. Philip reads one marker and suffers vertigo. He stops, checks the slope, water, and giant’s visible tracks, then asks the crew to correct a misleading map line. Father, unable to stand, shares the old road’s turns from memory.
- **Decision:** Philip delegates; he no longer treats independence as doing everything alone.
- **Magic:** The route echo is only one check among several. The cost remains active at the climax.
- **Clue / payoff:** F-06 and F-10; the old turn is physically passable.
- **Ending / link:** Aro sees the giant and raises his bow as it reaches the wall’s outer approach.

### 27. The Long Road Around

- **Setting / timeline:** Malcore’s east outer road, evening, Day 6.
- **Objective and conflict:** Prevent the giant from breaking the wall or being killed before it can choose the cleared route.
- **Important events:** City defenders form a line; Aro takes a clear shot. Philip points out that the giant keeps angling toward the dry rise. Father, carried to a safe observation point, does not order Philip away. Philip uses the knife once at a sound marker, accepts the dizzy cost, and directs workers to open the old turn. Aro lowers his bow long enough for the giant to see open ground. The giant pauses, tests the route, and turns east. No one approaches its head or tries to tame it.
- **Decision:** Father trusts Philip’s plan; Aro chooses not to shoot; the roadwarden orders evacuation and gate clearing.
- **Character payoff:** Philip’s strength is observation, communication, and accepting help. Father’s is restraint.
- **Climax:** The giant’s turn prevents a wall attack, but it is not yet safe until it reaches Stillstone Hollow.
- **Ending / link:** The group follows at a distance to ensure the route remains clear.

### 28. The Hollow Beyond

- **Setting / timeline:** Stillstone Hollow, then Malcore infirmary, Day 6 night and brief later coda.
- **Objective and conflict:** Confirm the giant has reached shelter, then settle the immediate family and civic consequences.
- **Important events:** The giant enters the hollow and remains there; the city posts a watch and agrees to maintain the old corridor. Father returns to treatment. He apologizes for withholding information and confusing fighting with strength. Philip says he wants to learn routekeeping and practical self-defense while returning to the shop, not joining a Slayer order.
- **Decision:** Philip chooses what parts of his father’s past to carry forward. Aro accepts that his friend’s courage does not require another boy to die the same way. Filroy leaves for his forest territory.
- **Resolution:** Father’s injury is stable but not healed; trust begins to repair; the city has a local route plan.
- **Final image:** Philip stands beyond Malcore’s gate looking at the road that leads back toward Hazelwood. He intends to travel it when Father can safely ride. His first journey past home was chosen, frightening, and his own.

## Continuity checks for drafting

- Keep five-day travel chronology and the order Hazelwood → crash ravine → forest/swamp → Malcore → east route → Stillstone Hollow.
- Father remains unable to walk after the crash; his clearer speech and judgment return gradually. A healer does not instantly cure him.
- Philip never knows the giant’s aim from the wayknife. He infers it from repeated route evidence, dry-ground choices, Filroy, and physical terrain.
- Aro does not fire at the giant before the climax; his raised bow is a genuine threat and his decision to lower it matters.
- Filroy helps with local terrain, not city maps or magical explanations.
- The giant survives and remains wild. The solution is passage and space, not control.
- Father and Philip do not settle every grievance in one apology. The final agreement begins a changed relationship.
