# 02 — Reimagining Plan: Approved Direction

## Author instruction

**NEW CANON:** The revised novel’s title is **The Road Beyond Hazelwood**. The source PDF retains its original title as part of its history. The new title points toward departure and change, but its exact thematic meaning remains open for the author to shape.

**NEW CANON — author-approved direction:** Combine **Direction A (The Survival Road)** with **Direction B (The Slayer’s Unfinished Road)**. Keep Philip’s survival journey and his care for his father at the center; make the father’s Slayer past, locked box, and dagger part of their present conflict.

**Author-requested exploration:** Leave room for magic and for a direct confrontation between Philip and his father. The specific magic rules, revelations, plot events, and ending developed in the Story Bible are **PLANNED** until established in manuscript prose or separately approved.

## The novel latent in the source

The strongest foundation is an intimate survival journey. Philip is not a trained adventurer: he is a bullied boy who likes commerce and familiar routines. The accident forces him to care for a father who once fought the very dangers Philip fears. The forest tests whether Philip can redefine strength through practical intelligence, persistence, and care. Filroy provides a relationship that is earned through action and conversation, while Malcore offers a destination that can open the story into a second phase.

The reimagining should preserve the source’s unusual, sometimes comic details and its sincere father–son center. It should not automatically turn the Slayer history into a prophecy, make Philip secretly destined for heroism, or make the giant part of a world-ending conspiracy. The story needs a clearer cause-and-effect spine, not more lore for its own sake.

## Direction selection

### Direction A — The Survival Road (selected foundation)

**Core promise:** A practical, frightened boy must get his injured father through a changing wilderness, learning that courage is not the same as becoming a fighter.

**Emphasis:** Philip’s decisions, physical limits, care for Father, incremental competence, the route between Hazelwood and Malcore, and an earned bond with Filroy. Aro’s world of hunters and the father’s past remain important but support the present journey rather than displacing it.

**Likely strengths:** Closest to the source’s emotional center and central action. It gives the title a clear journey meaning, makes Philip’s growth specific, and can deliver a complete ending without requiring epic-fantasy machinery.

**Main design challenge:** The father cannot remain unconscious as luggage. His recovery, limitations, and relationship with Philip need to affect the journey and its resolution. The route must also explain why Philip first plans to go north toward Hazelwood and ultimately reaches Malcore.

### Direction B — The Slayer’s Unfinished Road (selected strand)

**Core promise:** Philip’s journey exposes the unfinished consequences of his father’s years as a Slayer and forces them to decide what parts of that past deserve to be carried forward.

**Emphasis:** The locked box, the father’s private exchange with Aro, the old guild, the Cartivoree war, and the dagger. Philip’s value is not inherited combat talent; it comes from questioning the assumptions behind the Slayer tradition and choosing what kind of life to build.

**Likely strengths:** Gives the father’s history and the box a strong structural role and pays off several conspicuous mysteries from the source.

**Main design challenge:** The Slayer lore could overwhelm Philip’s grounded survival story or slide into familiar secret-legacy fantasy. Every revelation should change the father–son relationship or present danger, not merely add historical detail.

### Direction C — The Fox and the Forest (not selected as the main structure)

**Core promise:** Philip and Filroy become uneasy partners while the giant’s passage disrupts the lives and habitats of the forest’s inhabitants.

**Emphasis:** The fox’s motives, the relationship between people and creatures, the physical effects of the giant and storm, and the small negotiations needed to travel safely. Philip’s ability to notice needs and make useful bargains becomes his defining strength.

**Likely strengths:** Develops the source’s most distinctive companion and creature material and gives the forest more identity than a sequence of hazards.

**Main design challenge:** The father could lose his central emotional place, and the animal-community material could become episodic. The story would need a firm through-line that keeps Philip’s responsibility for Father at its center.

## Approved combination and development approach

The author selected a combination of A and B. Direction A supplies the structural spine; Direction B supplies the legacy conflict and mystery. Filroy and the forest remain important supporting elements from the source, without shifting the book into a fox-led ensemble story.

- Keep Philip’s practical survival choices, his father’s recovery, and their relationship at the heart of the plot.
- Make the old Slayer tradition a source of competing beliefs about strength, violence, responsibility, and protection.
- Develop restrained, rule-bound magic that grows from the source’s road, river, dwarven craft, and travel imagery. Do not make Philip a chosen one or give him an inherited combat gift.
- Give Philip and his father a substantial disagreement that changes through decisions and consequences, not one explanatory speech.
- Use Aro and the Alcrog story to test heroic reputation against the cost of a real death.
- Give Filroy a practical reason to help and a decision that matters to the climax or resolution.
- Resolve the father’s immediate danger, the giant’s threat, and Philip’s choice about what to carry forward from the Slayers.

The combination preserves what is most personal in the source: Philip is trying to save his father while becoming someone other than the fighter his father expects him to be. It also lets the title describe both the literal road and Philip’s first steps beyond the life his father has planned for him.
