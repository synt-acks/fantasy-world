# Chapter Fourteen

## The Route Past Home

The fork lay below the hill, where the north track split from the southbound road.

They descended slowly. Philip walked ahead with a branch, probing the leaf mold before the stretcher followed. The ground was drier along the slope, but the storm clouds had begun to thicken behind them. Every so often, a tremor ran beneath the path. It was too faint to knock anything loose, but Philip felt it in his sore hand where he leaned on the branch.

At a narrow shelf, the stretcher caught between two roots. Filroy braced the rear pole while Philip lifted the front. Father groaned. Philip froze until the sound passed.

“We should stop,” Filroy said.

“We can clear the root.”

“Your father needs stillness.”

Philip looked at the stretcher, then at the north track. They could stop in the open, or move six more paces to a patch of higher ground where the slope widened. He hated that every choice involved guessing what kind of trouble was worse.

“Six paces,” he said. “Then we stop.”

They moved the stretcher. When they reached the patch, Philip knelt beside Father and checked the leg wrap. The swelling had made the cloth tight. He loosened one strip and watched Father’s foot for color. The toes remained warm. Father’s breathing steadied, though he did not wake.

The small cup held only a thin film of water. Philip considered giving it to Father, then remembered that water alone would not fix the injury. He wet a scrap of cloth and touched it to Father’s lips. The gesture used almost nothing. It also did not pretend to solve anything.

Philip had seen the junction on the torn map, though the paper showed two thin lines and nothing of the ground between them. From the slope, he could see the north track climbing toward the place where the road had fallen away. The river beyond it was swollen and fast. The southern road curved between two low ridges, then disappeared into the trees.

The giant’s trail crossed both routes.

Philip lowered Father’s stretcher onto a patch of firm ground. Father’s eyes were closed, but he had begun to stir more often. His face was hot to the touch. The leg beneath the canvas had swollen further, and the skin above his boot had turned dark with bruising.

Philip took the torn map from his bag. It had dried into stiff folds, and one corner had split along a crease. He flattened it against a rock and traced the two routes with one finger. The north track returned to Hazelwood, where someone might help if they could reach the river. The south road reached Malcore, where a healer waited behind a wall. The map did not show the slide, the flood, or the storm. Maps were good at roads that stayed where they were drawn.

“We need to decide,” Philip said.

Filroy looked toward the north. “You already have.”

“I haven’t.”

“You keep looking south.”

Philip glanced at the flooded river. “I’m checking.”

“The river is still there.”

“It could go down.”

“It could.”

“And if we wait?”

Filroy’s ears moved toward the corridor. “The giant will keep moving.”

Philip knew the northern route better. The road home ran beside the river. If he could reach it, someone in Hazelwood could send help. There would be a bed for Father, clean water, and people who knew them. He could return to the shop and keep the rest of the world on the other side of the door.

The river had risen over its banks. The north track ended at a wall of fallen stone. The horses were gone. Father could not walk. Their food was gone, and what little water they had found was not enough.

Malcore was downstream. It had a healer. It was also the place Father had intended to reach, though he had not told Philip why.

Father moved beneath the blanket. His eyes opened, unfocused.

“Father?” Philip leaned over him. “Can you hear me?”

“The road?”

“North is blocked. The river is too high. We’re at a fork.”

Father swallowed. “Malcore.”

“That’s south.”

“Go south.”

“Why?”

Father’s eyes drifted shut. He did not answer.

Philip waited. The only thing that moved was Father’s breathing.

Philip held the cup near his father’s mouth. Father swallowed once, then turned away. Philip wiped the water from his chin with the edge of his sleeve. He wanted to ask more, but Father’s eyes had already closed. Philip had to decide with what he had: a broken road, a flooded river, a healer downstream, and a father who had said the same city twice.

“You can tell me why,” Philip said.

Father’s mouth shifted, but the words did not come. His eyes stayed closed.

The instruction had not made the choice for Philip. It had only confirmed that Father believed Malcore was the better place to go. Philip still had to decide if he agreed.

He looked north again. Home lay beyond the river and the slide. He could not reach it with Father on the stretcher. He could not leave Father to find help alone. He could not make the water safe by wanting it to be safe.

The south road was unfamiliar, but it was open. It ran toward the city and the healer. It was the only route that matched what they needed now.

“We’re going to Malcore,” Philip said.

Filroy watched him. “Because your father said so?”

“Because he needs a healer. Because the north road is gone. Because the river is dangerous.”

“And because you want to?”

Philip looked at the dark water. “I want to go home.”

“That wasn’t my question.”

Philip looked at the south road. “I’m choosing Malcore. It’s not the same as obeying him.”

Filroy nodded once. He did not tell Philip that the choice was right.

Philip tested the stretcher knots. He checked the little cup, the wrapped box, the dagger, and the torn map. The storm clouds had grown darker behind them. Rain would reach the forest soon.

They moved toward the south road.

The road was older than the trail they had followed. A strip of stone ran beneath the leaf litter, broken in places by roots. Worked markers stood at irregular intervals. Some leaned. Others had disappeared into moss and brush. Philip did not bring the wayknife near them. He used the direction of the river, the slope of the ground, and the line of stones to keep them moving south.

Father woke once as they crossed a shallow stream. Philip stopped the stretcher and gave him a sip from the cup. The water looked clear in the light, but tasted of stone and leaves.

“Malcore,” Father murmured.

“Yes.”

“Road Office.”

Philip leaned closer. “What about it?”

Father’s brow tightened. His mouth moved, but the words dissolved into breath. He fell asleep again.

Philip looked at Filroy. The fox gave no sign that he had understood.

“I’m not asking you to explain it,” Philip said.

“Good.”

The first drops of rain struck the leaves. The wind pressed through the trees, carrying a low rumble that might have been thunder or the distant movement of the giant.

Philip checked the map again and marked the fork with a small slash of charcoal. He did not know if he would need to explain the route to anyone. He wanted a record of where they had chosen, in case the storm or the dark made the road feel like somewhere else.

They were not yet at the old bridge.
