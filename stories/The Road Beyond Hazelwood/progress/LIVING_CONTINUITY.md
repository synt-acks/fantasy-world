# Living Continuity — *The Road Beyond Hazelwood*

## Manuscript state

- All 28 numbered chapter files follow the approved chapter order and have received a continuity and prose edit. Chapter 28 contains the ending. The current 42,580-word draft remains much shorter than the Story Bible’s 85,000–95,000-word target; substantive scene development is still needed if that full-novel target stands.
- The prose is close third person through Philip. No scene enters another character’s private thoughts.
- The title and combined A+B direction are author-approved. The survival journey and father–son conflict remain central; the Slayer history and magic press on that conflict without making Philip a chosen heir or recruiting him into a revived order.

## Chronology and geography

- Hazelwood lies north/upstream; Malcore is south/downstream; Stillstone Hollow lies east of Malcore.
- **Day 1:** Philip is bullied at the river dock and escapes with a rope after checking its iron bank ring. He tells Father the truth. Aro visits the shop, buys twelve elven arrows, and privately warns Father about the north road. Father advances the Malcore trip.
- **Day 2:** Philip and Father leave before dawn. The old Shelf Road collapses from erosion and earlier heavy ground pressure. Father pushes Philip onto an inner ledge; the wagon falls and burns. Both horses escape. Philip finds Father, opens the dwarven box, and notices the knife’s faint response at the cracked road fitting.
- **Day 3:** Philip gathers a small amount of berries, builds a stretcher, and finds the north road slide-blocked and the river unsafe. Father briefly directs him toward Malcore and a healer. Philip chooses the southbound route. Filroy begins helping. Philip misreads a strong marker trace and reaches a washed crossing. Father explains the basic wayknife rule while half-awake.
- **Day 4:** Philip tests a higher animal trail and crosses the swamp with Filroy. They find only a small amount of uncertain seep water. They see the giant from a distance and observe it following dry ground. Philip chooses Malcore at the fork. A severe natural storm breaks the bank at the old bridge and separates him from Father.
- **Day 5:** Filroy leads Philip to Father beneath the rooted bridge. Philip improvises a splint and shelter. Father explains Aro’s warning, the Road Office plan, the old Slayer use of roads, and his fear after Philip’s mother died in an orc raid while he was away. Aro catches up. The group observes the giant, compares old marker traces, identifies a possible eastward causeway, and camps near Malcore. Warning bells sound at first light.
- **Day 6:** Philip opens the gate wicket by easing its physical latch. Father receives ordinary medical care for a concussion and closed lower-leg fracture. The roadwarden authorizes limited inspection; Philip, Filroy, Aro, and roadworker Hedd uncover the old turn and a clogged drainage cut. Father and Philip’s strongest argument occurs in the infirmary. Workers open and test the route. The giant reaches the outer farms, turns east, and enters Stillstone Hollow. Father’s treatment and the road office’s route plan continue afterward.

## Philip

- Philip is fifteen, a shopkeeper’s son who prefers the shop to the forest and does not identify as a fighter. His strengths are practical attention, arithmetic, observation, and improvisation. He has no wilderness or Slayer training at the start.
- Before the journey he has bruises from the river bullying. After the crash he has a scalp cut, bruised right hand, bruised left shoulder, and mild concussion with dizziness. The storm causes another head impact. His fatigue, hunger, poor water supply, and hand pain remain consequential during the journey.
- He chooses to stay with Father after the crash and later chooses the southbound route himself. He accepts Filroy’s local knowledge and Aro’s help. He does not decide to become a Slayer.
- By the climax he can describe evidence and delegate, but he is still injured and suffers vertigo from a wayknife reading. In the coda weeks later, the scalp wound and hand injury are healing; no instant recovery is implied.

## Father and Philip

- Father is a widower and former Slayer. He loves Philip but tends to express fear as orders and to hide information to control risk.
- He has a head injury/concussion and a closed lower-leg fracture from the crash, worsened when the stretcher shifts in the storm. He cannot walk unaided through the trip. Malcore’s healer sets/stabilizes the leg with ordinary treatment; recovery takes weeks.
- Father’s wife died in an orc raid while he was away with the Slayers. He carries grief and guilt about his absence, not responsibility for causing the raid. No hidden magical cause or betrayal is revealed.
- Early arguments establish Philip’s need for information and agency. Father partially acknowledges that in Chapter 11, fully explains the mission and past in Chapter 17, repeats the controlling pattern at the Road Office and infirmary, then lets Philip keep the wayknife and help in Chapter 25. His apology begins repair but does not erase Philip’s anger.

## Aro and the Alcrog story

- Aro Silvet is a veteran hunter and archer in a weathered Alcrog hide. He travels to Malcore to report giant movement. He and Father had planned to meet there and consult the Road Office.
- Aro’s friend Philip killed an Alcrog from inside it but died from his wounds. The protagonist shares the name by coincidence only. There is no prophecy, kinship, or magical recognition.
- Aro remains offstage in the forest until he catches up in Chapter 18. He reaches the crash site after Philip and Father have left, then follows the stretcher marks and waits for the swamp water to fall. He carries a small amount of food and water. All twelve elven arrows bought in Hazelwood remain unused through the climax. At the ridge marker he borrows Philip’s single wayknife to read the trace.
- Aro believes hesitation can cost lives and initially favors a lethal fallback. In Chapter 21 he admits he has emphasized his friend’s victory while omitting fear and cost. He holds his shot in Chapter 27 after Philip shows the giant’s repeated eastward choices and the route is opened.
- Father and Aro once used a marker to draw a creature toward an empty quarry, hoping to keep it from a village. It turned before reaching the quarry; they moved the village in time but had not warned its people in advance. The incident illustrates how routekeeping can become control even when no one is hurt.

## Giant and route mystery

- The giant is solitary, nonverbal, house-high, dangerous, and not malicious. Its mountain shelf collapsed; it seeks familiar dry shelter at Stillstone Hollow. No character knows this at the beginning.
- It damages trees, roads, and farms because of its size. It follows a corridor, prefers high/dry ground, avoids a fox den and a flooded patch, reacts dangerously to a sudden clatter, and does not pursue Philip’s group.
- Modern barriers obscure the eastward turn toward Stillstone Hollow. Current heavy giant traffic can dominate the older marker trace and make its route look like a direct approach to Malcore. Its intention is inferred through tracks, terrain, behavior, Filroy, and Father’s route memory—not learned from magic.
- The giant survives and remains wild. People clear a path and evacuate the approach; they neither control nor tame it.

## Wayknife and Waykeeping

- The dwarven box was given to Father when he left the Slayers. Philip opened it after the crash with the key taken from Father’s vest. The key remains with Philip; the scorched box and dagger travel with him.
- The dagger is an ordinary blade with a limited ability to resonate near a sufficiently intact worked road marker. It reports the strongest repeated traffic as directional/rhythmic impressions; it cannot tell safety, intention, identity, destination, distance, or terrain beyond the marker.
- Cracked, rusted, shifted, flooded, or otherwise damaged markers can give weak or conflicting traces. Recent heavy passage can mask older routes. Readings warm and vibrate the hilt and create imbalance; repeated use brings nausea/vertigo and can cause a fall. The user must compare the impression with visible ground, tracks, slope, weather, and other people’s knowledge.
- Philip has read markers in Chapters 6, 10, 20, and 26–27. He makes a costly mistake at the washed crossing and experiences strong disorientation in the storm. At the climax he reads once at a sound marker, reports only the eastward trace, and relies on workers, Father’s memory, Filroy, and the physical route.
- Waykeeping does not cause the storm or road collapse, heal, teleport, control creatures, open locks, or grant combat strength. The gate wicket in Chapter 22 opens through mechanical observation alone.

## Filroy and supporting roles

- Filroy Foxman is a speaking red fox. His speech is an unexplained wonder separate from Waykeeping. He knows the local forest, dry ground, animal movement, and his own territory. He does not know the city’s archives and cannot carry Father or control the giant.
- Philip first calls him “Mr. Fox”; Filroy prefers Filroy and resists being ordered. He chooses to help because the giant damaged his territory and because their needs overlap. He returns to the forest at the end.
- Hedd is the roadworker who helps Philip inspect the old turn and drainage cut. The roadwarden remains unnamed. She is cautious and needs verifiable evidence, not a villain or a secret opponent.

## Objects, injuries, resources, and unresolved revision checks

- The stretcher is made from wagon branches, canvas, and harness webbing. It is repaired after the storm and remains necessary until Father reaches the infirmary. Track its location carefully in any expansion.
- The horses escaped; the wagon and most supplies burned. Philip has a small number of berries, scant uncertain water, a cup, limited cloth, a torn map, and Aro’s later food. Do not restore a convenient full pack.
- The iron fitting near the crash, road markers, causeway, drainage cut, gate wicket, and old farm barrier have physical construction and maintenance histories; magic does not repair or move them.
- Expansion pass should preserve the day-by-day order, Father’s impaired speech/mobility, Philip’s injuries, the limited resources, the giant’s nonverbal behavior, the fox’s independent motives, and the unresolved-but-improving father–son relationship.
- Chapters 13–28 remain especially compact and may need more dramatized scene development. Keep Aro’s first forest reappearance in Chapter 18; check gate/timeline transitions and wayknife use during any expansion. Do not add readings just to fill length.
