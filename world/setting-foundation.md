# Setting Foundation: Hazelwood, the Big River, and Malcore

## Purpose and authority

This is the first shared setting reference for the world introduced in *The Road Beyond Hazelwood*. It gathers details from the original source and the current revised manuscript so future stories have a place to begin. It is deliberately local: the source and draft do not yet define a complete continent, world map, or universal history.

The story files identify the revised title and the combined Survival Road / Slayer’s Unfinished Road direction as author-approved. The 28-chapter revised manuscript is still a working draft, and its continuity notes identify further development as outstanding. Source and draft details retain their labels; only decisions explicitly marked **NEW CANON** below are additions made in response to the author’s request to begin worldbuilding.

Use these labels when adding or revising information:

- **SOURCE:** Present in the original rough manuscript. Preserve as source material, but do not assume every detail survives the revision unchanged.
- **WORKING DRAFT:** Appears in the revised manuscript as it currently stands; not yet finalized or separately approved as shared canon.
- **PLANNED:** A development decision in the Story Bible or continuity plan that remains a plan where the prose has not established it.
- **OPEN:** The source and current working materials do not settle the question.
- **CANON:** Use only for facts explicitly approved by the author or established in finalized manuscript material.

For story-specific plot, character knowledge, and chapter decisions, use the [Story Bible](../stories/The%20Road%20Beyond%20Hazelwood/03_STORY_BIBLE.md) and [Living Continuity](../stories/The%20Road%20Beyond%20Hazelwood/progress/LIVING_CONTINUITY.md). This file is the cross-story setting seed, not a replacement for those documents.

## The setting at a glance

- **OPEN:** The wider world, continent, and political realm have no settled names in the available material. The novel can remain focused on the Hazelwood–Malcore corridor without naming them.
- **SOURCE:** The setting includes humans, elves, dwarves, orcs, trolls, centaurs, dragons, giants, and other unusual creatures. Their societies and relationships are mostly undescribed. Do not infer a single shared culture or political alignment for any people from the brief mentions.
- **SOURCE:** Everyday life shown or named includes family shops, school, books, docks, river traffic, wagons, horses, bows, and hand tools. Elven arrows and a dwarven-made box and dagger suggest distinct craft traditions, but the source does not define their economies or technologies in detail.
- **WORKING DRAFT:** The revised Hazelwood story introduces Malcore’s Road Office and route crews. The initial regional framework below establishes their shared-road mandate as new canon.

## The Hazelwood–Malcore corridor

| Place or feature | Established material and status |
|---|---|
| **Hazelwood** | **SOURCE:** A small town with a school, shops, and a dock on the Big River. Philip and his father run a shop. **WORKING DRAFT:** It lies north/upstream of Malcore and is Philip’s home. |
| **The Big River** | **SOURCE:** It begins at a mountain north of Hazelwood, passes through town, and runs south toward Malcore. The original journey’s directions do not consistently fit that geography. **WORKING DRAFT:** North is upstream toward Hazelwood; south is downstream toward Malcore. |
| **South Road / Shelf Road** | **PLANNED / WORKING DRAFT:** The revised story uses a southbound road corridor, including an old, failing Shelf Road near the crash site. Erosion, neglected drainage, and earlier heavy ground pressure contribute to its collapse; it is not a deliberate trap. |
| **Forest corridor** | **SOURCE:** Forest, swamp, mountain, cliff, and giant-damaged ground appear in the original. **WORKING DRAFT:** The forest lies along the route between Hazelwood and Malcore; old markers, wet crossings, and a giant’s passage affect travel. It is inhabited terrain, not a uniform wilderness. |
| **Malcore** | **SOURCE:** A walled city with a stepped approach, gate, corner towers, and a gold central tower where its leader lives. **WORKING DRAFT:** It is downstream/south of Hazelwood and has civic road staff, a council, and ordinary medical care. Whether it belongs to a wider kingdom or federation remains open. |
| **Stillstone Hollow** | **PLANNED / WORKING DRAFT:** A dry basalt shelter east of Malcore, used as the giant’s destination in the revised story. It is new to the reimagining, not a fact recovered from the original source. |
| **Other named regions** | **SOURCE:** The Twin Mountains lie east, and the desert of Cirm is mentioned. Their positions, borders, inhabitants, and relationship to the Hazelwood corridor remain undescribed. |

The original draft contains a genuine route ambiguity: Philip says he will follow the river north toward Hazelwood, but the story ends with him at Malcore, which the same material places downstream to the south. The revised manuscript gives him a blocked return route and a reason to choose Malcore. Keep the revised compass convention internally consistent; do not treat the original mismatch as a hidden magical effect.

No reliable regional map or measured distances exist yet. The revised story’s travel times are working continuity for that novel, not a settled scale for the larger world.

## Initial regional framework

The Big River corridor is the first defined region of the setting. It is organized around two kinds of movement: the river carries bulk trade downstream, while the road connects communities and remains usable where the river cannot take a traveler. The region has no new proper name; “Big River corridor” is a descriptive label.

**NEW CANON — author-requested creation:** River and road form one shared regional lifeline, and the communities along them coordinate public infrastructure without requiring a single empire or a centralized authority over everyday town life.

### Relative map

Not to scale. Placements follow the revised story’s **WORKING DRAFT** geography; distances and exact banks remain unsettled.

```text
                              NORTH / UPSTREAM
                                    ↑
                           mountain river source
                                    │
WEST / across river       BIG RIVER       EAST / forest
                                    │      Hazelwood and dock
                                    │      South Road
                                    │      Shelf Road, swamp, bridge
                                    │      Malcore and its gate
                                    │             └── eastward route ── Stillstone Hollow
                                    ↓
                              SOUTH / DOWNSTREAM
```

The Twin Mountains and desert of Cirm remain beyond this regional sketch. Their locations are too vague to place at a measured distance or connect to a border.

### Settlements and their relationship

- **SOURCE:** Hazelwood is a small river town with a dock, shops, and a school. **NEW CANON:** Its merchants serve local households and passing travelers; town life is locally managed. No mayor, council, or charter is established.
- **SOURCE:** Malcore is a walled city with a gate and central tower. **WORKING DRAFT:** It is the corridor’s downstream trade hub, with a Road Office and road crews. **NEW CANON:** That office maintains shared roads, gates, and public markers and coordinates public-safety responses; it does not routinely control Hazelwood’s shop, school, or local decisions.
- **NEW CANON:** Hazelwood and Malcore cooperate over shared infrastructure and trade. Their relationship is practical rather than imperial. Whether they belong to a larger kingdom or federation remains **OPEN**.

### Trade and travel

- **NEW CANON:** Barges and other river craft carry heavy goods downstream toward Malcore. Returning upstream takes more labor or time, so the south road remains important for travelers and goods moving north.
- Hazelwood’s shop is part of a mixed local and traveler economy. **SOURCE:** It sells ordinary supplies, and Aro buys elven arrows there. The wider range of goods, prices, and trading partners remains **OPEN**.
- **PLANNED:** Malcore records gate tolls as an official measure of taxable traffic. Those records help the city understand which public routes serve people and commerce. Exact rates and how revenue is divided are not established.
- **WORKING DRAFT:** The maintained road follows the east side of the river south from Hazelwood. Road crews prioritize the active route, bridges, drainage, and gate approaches. Older side routes receive less attention as traffic shifts; this explains how a worked marker or old causeway can remain physically present while disappearing from current maps.
- Travel reports move by people, posted notices, and warning bells. There is no instant long-distance communication. A report can arrive late or lose detail between Hazelwood and Malcore.

### Shared obligations and local limits

**NEW CANON:** The road is a public lifeline, but knowing where it goes is not the same as knowing whether it is safe. Towns report washouts, damaged markers, and dangerous traffic; Malcore’s road staff inspect and prioritize repairs. During an immediate threat, officials can close a road, warn travelers, and organize an evacuation along the public route. Private land and local decisions remain with the people who live there unless a specific emergency agreement says otherwise.

The old Slayer practice of diverting dangerous creatures sits uneasily against this arrangement. A route decision can protect one settlement while transferring danger to another. The current foundation therefore treats warning and local knowledge as part of responsible route work; the Slayers’ former authority does not automatically give them the right to decide for a community.

### Texture for future stories

- River direction is part of ordinary practical knowledge: south is downstream toward Malcore; north is upstream toward Hazelwood.
- People judge a road by its drainage, surface, bridges, and upkeep as much as by its marks on a map.
- Hazelwood can feel intimate without making every resident agree; Malcore can feel formal without making its officials uncaring.
- The corridor’s useful conflicts are local and material: who maintains a bridge, whose warning is believed, who pays for a repair, and who bears the risk when a route changes.
- This framework does not define a universal calendar, regional language, ethnic map, ruler, or total trade economy. Add those details only when a story needs them.

## History, hunters, and civic life

### The Slayers and the Cartivoree war

- **SOURCE:** The Slayers were a beast-hunting guild. They helped defeat the dragon Cartivoree and its orc army; raids killed Philip’s mother. The Slayers later disbanded.
- **SOURCE:** Philip’s father is a former Slayer. Aro Silvet is a celebrated hunter associated with dangerous creatures. The original does not explain the guild’s structure, reach, or reasons for dissolving.
- **WORKING DRAFT / PLANNED:** The reimagining develops the Slayers’ work to include route knowledge and argues over how hunters should use it. The guild remains disbanded; its history is not evidence of a hidden surviving order.
- **WORKING DRAFT:** Philip’s mother died in an orc raid while his father was away. The draft treats his grief and guilt about his absence as personal, not as proof he caused the raid. No secret magical cause is established.

The war, raid, and dissolution are part of the source’s past, but the date, campaign geography, political aftermath, and orc societies are **OPEN**. The revised Story Bible proposes a more specific timeline; use it as story planning until the manuscript is finalized.

### Local institutions

- **SOURCE:** Hazelwood has a school, family businesses, and a working dock. Aro buys elven arrows from Philip’s shop.
- **WORKING DRAFT:** Malcore’s Road Office and road crews maintain current routes and investigate old markers; a roadwarden coordinates a local emergency. These are civic institutions introduced by the reimagining, not yet evidence of a world-spanning road authority.
- **OPEN:** The region’s higher government, laws outside public roads, trade boundaries, and exact division of toll revenue have not been established.
- **OPEN:** “Unkati” is used as a day or observance associated with Philip running the shop. Its exact meaning and customs are undecided.

## Creatures and peoples

The source names creatures more often than it explains them. Preserve that limited knowledge rather than filling in unapproved biology or culture.

- **Forgons — SOURCE:** Thumb-sized creatures that eat fungus and smaller animals.
- **Tilfors — SOURCE:** Large, horned creatures.
- **Alcrogs — SOURCE:** Flying predators compared to wolves and eagles. Aro’s story describes a person killing one from inside it and dying afterward from his injuries.
- **Giants — SOURCE:** A giant has left the mountain and entered the forest; the original giant has a rock-hard head and can be wounded at the shoulder and neck. **WORKING DRAFT:** The revised giant is solitary, nonverbal, and dangerous because of its size and defensive reactions. Its destination and displacement cause are story-specific draft additions.
- **Foxes — SOURCE:** Filroy Foxman is a fox who speaks with Philip and helps him. **OPEN:** Why he speaks, whether other animals can, and how common this is are unexplained. Keep his speech separate from any draft-specific road magic unless the author decides otherwise.
- **Elves, dwarves, orcs, trolls, centaurs, and dragons — SOURCE:** Named in the original, with only limited detail. Their cultures, languages, political organization, and present-day relations remain open except where an individual story establishes them.

The original storm includes a bright streak striking the ground. It does not explain the event as magic. The revised story treats its storm as severe weather; do not use the unexplained image alone to establish a broader magical rule.

## Magic and craft

### What the original establishes

**SOURCE:** The fox speaks; the dwarven dagger is finely made; unfamiliar creatures have unusual traits; and the storm includes an unexplained flash. The original does not give a coherent magic system or state which of these are magical. In particular, it does not establish that the dagger has supernatural abilities.

### Waykeeping in the revised story

**WORKING DRAFT / PLANNED:** The revised novel develops the dagger into a dwarven wayknife that can read repeated passage at sufficiently intact worked road markers. This is new design based on the story’s roads, tools, and travel motifs, not recovered source lore.

Its current rules are narrow: it gives a local directional and rhythmic impression, not identity, safety, intent, distance, destination, or a view of terrain ahead. Recent heavy traffic can obscure older traces; damaged markers can be weak or conflicting. A reading briefly disrupts balance, and repeated use causes nausea or disorientation. The user must check the impression against physical tracks and other people’s knowledge. The knife remains an ordinary blade. It cannot heal, teleport, compel a creature, open a lock, or win a fight by magic.

The detailed version belongs in the [Story Bible’s Waykeeping section](../stories/The%20Road%20Beyond%20Hazelwood/03_STORY_BIBLE.md#6-magic-system-rules--waykeeping). It remains draft-level setting design until approved or established in finalized prose. Filroy’s speech is an independent, unexplained wonder in the current material.

## Open development questions

These are useful next questions, not decisions to settle by inference:

1. What geography is necessary beyond the Big River corridor, and what map scale can support it?
2. Does a wider kingdom or federation include both towns, and how are toll revenues divided?
3. Which details of the Cartivoree war and Slayer history matter outside Philip’s family story?
4. What, if anything, do “elf,” “dwarf,” “orc,” and the named creatures mean as cultures or kinds of beings in this world?
5. Is Waykeeping a local craft, one example of a wider magic system, or the only magic this story needs? How rare is Filroy’s speech?
6. What does “Unkati” mean in Hazelwood, and should its customs matter beyond the shop scenes?

Resolve these when another story or a concrete scene needs them. Until then, keep them **OPEN** rather than adding lore for completeness alone.
