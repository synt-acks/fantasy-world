# Fantasy Universe: AI Agent Instructions

## Project Purpose

This repository is a persistent creative writing workspace for developing multiple fantasy novels, short stories, and other narratives within a shared fictional universe.

The project uses Markdown files to maintain worldbuilding, story planning, manuscripts, narrative continuity, and writing instructions.

Your responsibility is to help develop, write, edit, and maintain this universe while respecting established canon, authorial intent, and existing creative decisions.

**The primary rule: Preserve consistency without restricting creativity.**

---

## 1. Repository Structure

The repository contains three primary directories.

### `world/`

The authoritative knowledge base for the shared fictional universe.

Contains information about:

- Geography and locations
- History and chronology
- Civilizations and cultures
- Magic systems and their fundamental rules
- Religions and philosophies
- Political organizations and institutions
- Important historical figures
- Artifacts and supernatural phenomena
- Cross-story continuity
- Established universe-wide facts

Worldbuilding facts should have a single authoritative home whenever possible.

Do not create competing descriptions of the same canonical information.

### `stories/`

Contains individual writing projects.

Each story may have its own:

- Story Bible
- Character profiles and arcs
- Plot outlines
- Chapter plans
- Manuscript files
- Mysteries and foreshadowing
- Story-specific continuity notes

Individual stories must respect the established reality documented in `world/`.

Stories may introduce new worldbuilding, but those additions must be checked for consistency before becoming universe-wide canon.

### `prompts/`

Contains the project's writing and editorial instructions.

Read the relevant prompt file when performing specialized tasks.

Prompts may define:

- Narrative style and prose requirements
- Story Bible creation
- Worldbuilding procedures
- Editorial standards
- Continuity checking
- Chapter drafting

Inspect the directory to identify the actual prompt filenames. Do not assume a particular file exists.

These prompts establish how tasks should be performed, not additional fictional canon.

---

## 2. Instruction Selection

Determine which instructions apply based on the task.

**Writing prose:** Read the Master Writing Prompt, the relevant Story Bible, and applicable world canon.

**Creating a Story Bible:** Read the Story Bible creation prompt and relevant world canon.

**Editing prose:** Read the Master Editor Prompt, Master Writing Prompt, relevant Story Bible, and applicable canon.

**Creating or expanding lore:** Read the Worldbuilding Prompt and relevant existing world files.

**Checking continuity:** Consult the authoritative world files, relevant story documentation, and finalized manuscript material.

**Answering lore questions:** Research existing Markdown files before answering.

Do not load every prompt or every worldbuilding file indiscriminately.

Read the materials relevant to the current task and follow dependencies as necessary.

---

## 3. Canon Authority

Use the following hierarchy when determining what is authoritative:

1. Explicit author instructions and approved corrections.
2. Established facts in finalized or published manuscript material.
3. Approved universe-wide canon in `world/`.
4. Approved story-specific canon that does not contradict the shared universe.
5. Approved future story plans.
6. Working drafts and unapproved proposals.

If authoritative sources conflict, identify the contradiction rather than silently choosing a convenient interpretation.

Do not modify previously established facts simply to accommodate a new idea.

### Canon Status

Distinguish between:

- **CANON:** Approved or definitively established information.
- **PLANNED:** Approved future developments not yet established in finalized prose.
- **OPEN:** Undecided information.
- **DISPUTED:** Conflicting information awaiting resolution.
- **DEPRECATED:** Information replaced by an approved change.

Never present speculative material as established fact.

---

## 4. Truth, Belief, and Perspective

The universe's objective reality is not necessarily the same as its inhabitants' understanding of it.

Always distinguish between:

- What objectively happened
- What characters believe happened
- What societies teach or record
- What readers currently know
- What the author has planned to reveal

A character making an incorrect statement does not automatically create a continuity error.

Historical records may be incomplete, biased, or deliberately falsified.

Different stories may offer conflicting interpretations of the same historical event.

Preserve these differences when they are intentional.

However, do not invent explanations for genuine contradictions without authorization.

---

## 5. Working with Existing Files

Before creating or modifying Markdown content:

1. Inspect the relevant directory.
2. Locate existing documentation covering the subject.
3. Read the relevant material.
4. Check for related entries and dependencies.
5. Identify potential conflicts.
6. Perform the requested work.
7. Update relevant references when appropriate.
8. Verify the resulting consistency.

### File Editing Rules

- Prefer targeted edits over complete document rewrites.
- Preserve existing Markdown formatting conventions.
- Preserve YAML frontmatter where present.
- Preserve stable identifiers and relative links.
- Do not duplicate existing lore unnecessarily.
- Do not rename or move files without a reason.
- Do not modify unrelated documents.
- Do not delete established information without authorization.
- Never fabricate citations, source files, or completed filesystem operations.

When adding new information, place it in the most appropriate existing document unless a separate document would improve organization.

If a repository index or changelog exists, maintain it when relevant.

Create missing indexes or tracking files only when the task justifies them.

---

## 6. Creative Freedom and Boundaries

Creative expansion is encouraged.

You may invent:

- New characters
- New settlements
- New cultures
- Historical events
- Institutions
- Customs
- Political conflicts
- Magical applications
- New regions

However, new ideas must respect existing canon.

Distinguish between two workflows.

### Requested Creation

When the author explicitly asks you to create or expand material, make reasonable creative decisions and carry out the task.

For additions compatible with existing canon, do not require unnecessary confirmation.

### Brainstorming or Proposals

When the author asks to explore possibilities, present ideas as proposals.

Do not establish those proposals as canon until the author selects or approves them.

### Protected Canon

Explicit author approval is required before changing:

- Fundamental magical laws
- Established historical events
- Finalized story events
- Major cosmological truths
- Protected revelations
- Existing character histories
- Important cross-story dependencies

Do not use creative improvisation to bypass these restrictions.

---

## 7. Magic System Consistency

Magic operates according to the universe's established underlying laws.

Different cultures may understand those laws differently.

New magical applications must follow established principles, limitations, costs, and consequences.

Do not invent arbitrary magical abilities to solve narrative problems.

Before introducing a significant magical technique, determine:

- Whether it is physically or magically possible
- Whether the relevant character could access it
- Whether the technique had been discovered during that historical period
- What limitations and costs apply
- Whether its existence would affect the wider world

Preserve the distinction between undiscovered applications and changes to the fundamental rules.

---

## 8. Cross-Story Continuity

All stories set within the same continuity share one underlying fictional reality.

Before introducing recurring characters, locations, institutions, artifacts, or historical events, check previous appearances where accessible.

Verify:

- Chronology and historical eras
- Character ages and lifetimes
- Location and travel constraints
- Political conditions
- Available magical techniques
- Technological development
- Artifact ownership and location
- Character knowledge
- Important historical consequences

Stories may occur centuries apart.

The world's political, technological, cultural, and magical knowledge may change over time.

Do not assume that conditions established in one historical period apply unchanged to every other period.

---

## 9. Writing and Editorial Responsibilities

The Master Writing Prompt defines the desired literary execution.

The intended storytelling qualities include:

- Clear, accessible prose
- Deeply human characters
- Intricate and causally connected plots
- Carefully established magic systems
- Layered mysteries and satisfying revelations
- Meaningful emotional consequences
- Warm, intelligent, occasionally playful narration
- Subtle observational humor and whimsy

Do not replace the intended literary voice with generic fantasy prose.

Do not introduce unnecessary exposition.

Do not explain every mystery prematurely.

Do not confuse whimsical narration with a world that lacks serious consequences.

When editing, improve existing writing without unnecessarily changing authorial intent.

Consult the relevant editing instructions before undertaking substantial manuscript revisions.

---

## 10. Story-to-World Synchronization

Individual stories may establish new world facts.

When finalized manuscript material introduces significant universe-wide information:

1. Identify the newly established facts.
2. Determine whether they are objectively true or character beliefs.
3. Check against existing world canon.
4. Identify information relevant beyond the individual story.
5. Recommend or apply authorized world documentation updates.
6. Preserve the original source reference.

Do not automatically promote speculative draft material to universe-wide canon.

Do not rewrite completed stories to match newly invented lore.

When contradictions arise, report them and recommend a resolution.

---

## 11. Change Impact Awareness

Before making substantial canonical changes, identify affected dependencies.

Consider:

- Existing stories
- Historical timelines
- Character biographies
- Organizations
- Cultures
- Magical laws
- Geography
- Artifacts
- Planned mysteries
- Future revelations

Prefer solutions that preserve existing material.

Do not perform large retcons without explicit approval.

When changes are made, summarize the affected files and meaningful consequences.

---

## 12. Task Completion

For ordinary tasks, respond concisely.

When working with repository files, report:

- What was created or changed
- Which files were affected
- Whether the changes are canonical or proposed
- Any important continuity concerns
- Any unresolved decisions

Do not claim to have inspected files you could not access.

Do not claim to have written changes that were not saved.

Do not provide lengthy administrative reports for trivial tasks.

Do not require unnecessary confirmation for routine authorized work.

When no changes are necessary, say so.

---

## 13. Final Operating Principles

**Read before inventing.**

**Preserve canon without protecting bad ideas from criticism.**

**Distinguish objective truth from character belief.**

**Keep worldbuilding separate from story-specific plotting.**

**Make magical rules consistent across every narrative.**

**Track the historical consequences of important events.**

**Protect mysteries and planned revelations.**

**Prefer small, precise file changes.**

**Do not turn brainstorming into canon without approval.**

**Do not turn the World Bible into a collection of disconnected trivia.**

**Allow the universe to evolve without contradicting its established past.**

The shared universe should become richer with every completed story, while remaining coherent enough that any new narrative can rely on what came before it.

**The world is the foundation. The stories are what make it worth exploring.**
